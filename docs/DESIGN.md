# Design: how moves are judged, explained, scheduled and rewarded

Everything here lives in `shared/` (used by both the browser and the server),
so what you see is exactly what gets recorded.

## 1. Ground truth: Stockfish and master games, computed ahead of time

The endgame site judges moves with a tablebase. Openings have no tablebase,
and a language model would invent moves and evaluations (see
[RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md)). So `scripts/build-course.mjs` does the work once,
when the course is built:

* **Every legal Black move** in every position of every line is evaluated by
  Stockfish 16 (all moves at depth 12, the best three and the repertoire
  move at depth 16). The site can then judge any move in the course
  instantly, with no engine running. (Sparring rounds leave the course, so
  they run Stockfish in the browser: see section 8.)
* **Refutation lines** (Stockfish's principal variation) are stored for the 25
  most plausible moves in each position and every move the course mentions.
* **Masters statistics** for every position come from the Lichess masters
  database.
* **Your opponents' moves** in every position come from ham_sandmitch's games.

The build fails if a line is illegal, uses an unknown principle tag, has no
plan question, or gives two different moves for the same position (which can
happen when lines transpose). It warns when Stockfish rates a repertoire move
more than 0.45 pawns below its best. Three such moves remain, all established
theory: the Mar del Plata's 11...Nf6, the King's Indian set-up against 2.Nc3
(Stockfish prefers ...d5), and the Sämisch's 6...e5 (it prefers the ...c5
gambit).

## 2. Judging a move in a line (`shared/judge.js`)

Losses are measured in **winning chances**, with Lichess's formula
`2/(1+e^(-0.00368·cp)) − 1`, so half a pawn matters more in a level position
than when you're a piece up. The loss is measured against the repertoire
move, so nothing better than the move we teach is ever called a mistake.

| Your move | Grade | Counts as a miss? |
|---|---|---|
| The repertoire move | ✓ Repertoire move | No |
| Within 0.04 of the repertoire move, or played in 50+ master games and within 0.08 | ≈ Good move, but not your repertoire ("try again") | No |
| Loses 0.04-0.1 | ?! Not quite | Yes |
| Loses 0.1-0.2 | ?! Inaccuracy | Yes |
| Loses 0.2-0.3 | ? Mistake | Yes |
| Loses 0.3 or more | ?? Blunder | Yes |

The thresholds above 0.1 are Lichess's (inaccuracy 0.1, mistake 0.2, blunder 0.3).

**After a miss:** the first try explains what went wrong but doesn't give the
answer (retrieval practice). The second try reveals the repertoire move with
a green arrow and its note. The board never plays the wrong move; arrows show
your move (orange) and White's refutation (red).

**Hints:** 💡 *Idea* shows the principle behind the repertoire move, not the
move (−10% points, ¾ rating credit). 👁 *Show move* draws it (−25% points, half
a clove, half rating credit).

## 3. Explaining a move (`shared/explain.js`)

Two layers, both generated by rules on top of the stored Stockfish lines, so
they can't misstate the position:

**What happens (concrete):**
* *It allows mate:* the refutation ends in mate ("9.Nxc7+ Kf8 10.Rd8#").
* *White wins a pawn / a piece / the exchange:* the line is played out and the
  material balance is read after the recaptures settle.
* *A pawn kicks your knight* / *attacks two things at once:* White's first
  reply is checked for kicks and forks.
* *You could have won …:* the best move's line wins material that your move
  didn't.
* *It leaves a pawn loose:* no stored line, but your move leaves something
  that White can simply take.
* Otherwise: "No material changes hands in Stockfish's line, but it rates the
  move about N pawns worse than the best move here".

**Why (the principle):** the first rule that applies.
1. Material lost or missed, or mate allowed → *Checks, captures, threats*.
2. A move by your kingside pawns or king while the king lives there → *Leave
   your king's pawn shield alone*.
3. A slow move when the repertoire or best move is a pawn break → *Pawn
   breaks are your counterplay* (or *Against quiet systems, strike before
   you're squeezed* in the London, Colle and quiet c4 chapters).
4. An early queen move, a retreat to the back rank → *Develop, castle, then
   fight*.
5. Moving the g7 bishop off its diagonal → *The g7 bishop is your best piece*.
6. White's reply kicks the f6 knight → *Don't let e4-e5 kick your knight*.
7. Otherwise the principle tagged on the repertoire move.

A note written for that exact wrong move in the course (a PGN variation such
as *6...h6 waiting move*, *5...dxe5 the trap you fell into twice*) always
comes first.

## 4. Surprise mistakes

At each White move along a line, the build looks for White moves that hand
Black at least 1.3 pawns and leave Black at least +0.9. It keeps the two most
natural ones:

* **Preferred:** moves your real opponents played in that position, castling,
  developing a knight or bishop, central pawn moves.
* **Excluded:** king walks, retreats to the back rank, and a piece simply
  dropped where it can be taken for free (too obvious to train anything).
  The exception is a move your opponents really played.

The punishing moves are all moves within 0.6 pawns of Black's best that keep
at least +0.9.

**In practice:** a practice run of a line you have already got right once has
a 25% chance of a surprise. You can change the rate in Settings: never,
rarely, sometimes or often. Check-ups never have one. You aren't warned until
the move appears. Your answer is graded *punished* (one of the punishing
moves), *partial* (still +0.8 and within 0.2) or *missed*, and the run ends
there.

A surprise run never counts as the line's test for the day, so the clean test
still comes later. "⚡ A line with a surprise" on the Train page forces one.

## 5. Opening School (`shared/curriculum.js`)

* **Now learning:** the lines you have started, plus new ones in course order
  (the systems you meet most come first), up to 3 at a time. Lines you're
  struggling with (under 60% clean over 2+ tries) come first.
* **Learn first:** a line you have never played opens as a lesson. The run
  right after it ("Play it yourself") is from memory, so it can be the day's
  test, but it doesn't change your rating.
* **The test:** the first run of a line each day. Clean (every move right;
  "good but not your repertoire" moves allowed) = a learned day; a miss resets
  the count to 0. Later runs that day are practice.
* **Graduation:** 3 learned days. Check-ups follow after 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60
  days, at most 6 a day, with a **per-move clock** (5 s by default; 3, 8 or 12 s
  or off in Settings).
  * A clean check-up moves on to the next interval.
  * A check-up with a move over the clock repeats the same interval.
  * A miss sends the line back to learning with one learned day kept.
* **Deck positions** (your mistakes and your opponents' mistakes from your
  games) are interleaved: one after every two lines while any are due, up to 6
  a day.
  * A right answer brings the position back after 3, 10 and 30 days, then it
    is mastered.
  * A wrong one brings it back tomorrow.
  * An answer counts if Stockfish rates it within 0.05 winning chances of the
    best move.
* **Sparring rounds** (section 8): one after every three other exercises, two
  a day.
* When everything is done, School serves extra practice, with a sparring round
  after every three.

## 6. Points and the Garlick Path, cloves, rating

**Points move you along the Garlick Path** (`shared/path.js`), a map like a
puzzle game's:
* **Stages and worlds:** stages 1-1, 1-2 … 1-10, then world 2 starts at 2-1.
* **Points per stage:** 100 in world 1, 150 in world 2, +50 per world after
  that. Stage 1-2 is at 100 points, world 2 at 1,000, world 3 at 2,500.
* **Themes:** each world has its own name, colours and scenery. In order:
  Garden Gate 🌱, Sprout Meadow 🌿, Bulb Hollow 🧄, Braid Market 🧺,
  Kitchen Kingdom 🍳, Castle Larder 🏰, Midnight Grove 🌙, Dragon Peaks 🐉,
  Golden Harvest 🌾, Garlick Crown 👑.
* **After world 10** the themes repeat as "II", "III"….
* **Points only go up**, so the path only moves forward.

**Points per exercise** (`shared/scoring.js`):

| | |
|---|---|
| Completed | Base × difficulty. Base: a line 8 + 2 per Black move (a surprise run: per move played); a punish position 15; one of your mistakes 12. Difficulty by the exercise's rating: Easy (≤950) ×1, Medium (≤1150) ×1.25, Hard (≤1300) ×1.5, Expert ×2. Timed check-ups add 75 to the rating |
| Finished a line with misses | 40% of that, one star |
| Clean | +25% (no misses, no hints) |
| Speed | Up to +30%, measured by thinking time per move (only while it's your turn). Full bonus at half of par or less; nothing at par. Par is 6 s a move for lines, 40 s for positions from your games |
| Combo | +10% for each clean run in a row today before this one, up to +50% |
| Surprise punished | +50% |
| Hints | −10% per idea hint, −25% per move shown; a finished exercise is worth at least 20% of its completion points |
| Full bulb | 50 points + 10 per day of the streak you bring into today (up to 150) |
| Golden clove | An occasional bonus (about 1 in 8 at your level) |
| Lesson replay | Half points |
| Stars | ⭐ finished · ⭐⭐ clean · ⭐⭐⭐ clean and fast (at least half the speed bonus) |

**Cloves:**
* 1 for the day's test of a line, a due check-up, or a deck position.
* ½ for extra practice.
* 1 for the run after a lesson (up to 2 a day).
* A shown move halves it.

The bulb, 🛡️ streak shields woven from extra cloves, repair with a double
bulb, and golden cloves follow the same rules as the endgame site.

**The opening rating is separate from points.** It's Elo-style and starts at
your chess rating, gently anchored to it. It moves up and down with results;
points don't affect it.
* Line ratings by chapter: tricks 800, London and Colle 1000, flank 1050,
  quiet c4 and Bg5 systems 1100, Jobava 1150, main lines 1250, plus 5 per move
  beyond five.
* Deck positions are rated 1050-1350.
* Clean runs never lower it, and runs right after a lesson are unrated.

## 7. Your games (`client/src/course.ts`, `server/chesscom.js`)

The server fetches the last two months of your chess.com games (cached for 10
minutes) and the browser compares each game with the repertoire **by
position**. A game that reaches a course position by another move order counts
("transposed into your repertoire"). For each game you see:

* where it left the course,
* who left first,
* if it was you, how Stockfish rates your move,
* and a button to drill the line.

## 8. Sparring rounds (`shared/sparring.js`, `client/src/game/useSparring.ts`)

Lines teach the repertoire; sparring tests it against the moves you'll really
meet, slips included.

* **White plays like club players.** Each White move is drawn from the
  Lichess database (players rated 1000–1800, blitz and rapid) in proportion to
  how often club players chose it in that position. Moves played in under 1%
  of the games are left out. On move 1, White picks among the first moves the
  course covers (1.d4, 1.c4, 1.Nf3...). The site shows how common White's
  move is ("Bf4 is played in 41% of 12,345 club games here").
* **You play Black.**
  * In a course position (by position, so transpositions count) the move is
    judged like a line. The repertoire move is right. Another good move asks
    you to try again, without counting a miss.
  * Off the course, Stockfish 19 lite runs in your browser (WebAssembly, about
    2 MB, served by the site) while you think: the 3 best moves, depth 15 or
    2.5 seconds. If you play something else, it searches that move too. A move
    within 0.08 winning chances of the best is fine. Anything worse is graded
    (inexact, inaccuracy, mistake, blunder) and explained with the same rules
    as the course (`explain.js`, from the engine's lines).
  * A miss explains; the second one shows the move.
* **White's slips.** After every White move, the engine compares the
  position with the one before. White's move is a slip when it gives away
  at least 0.12 of White's winning chances and leaves you at least +0.2:
  * ?! from 0.12, ? from 0.2, ?? from 0.3.
  * You aren't told. Your first answer counts as punishing it if it's within
    0.05 of the best move.
  * Afterwards the move list marks the slip, and the round's summary lists
    every slip with your answer and the best one.
* **The round ends** after 12 of your moves, when you're 3.5 pawns up, when
  fewer than 25 club games reach White's position, or when the game is over.
* **Points:** like a line of the same length at "Hard" difficulty, +30% for
  each slip punished (up to 3). A finished round with misses earns 40%, plus
  the slip bonus. A clean round fills a clove. Rounds beyond the day's two
  fill half a clove. Rounds don't change the opening rating, because White's
  moves are different every time.
* **Badges:** Sparring Partner (finish a round) and Club Crusher (punish 10
  slips).
* Without a Lichess token on the server, the masters database stands in.
  Rounds then end sooner, because masters' games thin out quickly.
